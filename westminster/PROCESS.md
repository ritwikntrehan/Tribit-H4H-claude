# Westminster Development Review: application process

**Scenario:** hypothetical four-unit new residential development. This is a planning-stage roadmap, not an assurance of entitlement or permit eligibility. Source: [Westminster's official process](https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/community-development/planning-division/development-review-process) and [Development Review packet](https://www.westminster-ca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/536/637544150393130000).

## 1. Pre-submittal / entitlement scoping

Contact Planning Division at **(714) 548-3247** or **planning@westminster-ca.gov**. Supply the parcel/APN, proposed unit count, zoning, site description, and preliminary plans. Ask staff to confirm the correct entitlement(s), whether General Land Use and Development, Density Bonus, SB 330 replacement housing, CEQA materials, public notification, subdivision/engineering, or other filings are needed. Ask whether any ministerial or streamlined route applies; do not assume a discretionary Development Review path without checking.

## 2. Assemble the planning application

Complete the Development Review application and owner authorization, detailed project narrative, housing energy questionnaire, hazardous waste statement, ownership evidence, current full title report, project drawings, and other required/conditional items in [SUBMITTAL-CHECKLIST.md](SUBMITTAL-CHECKLIST.md). Have the responsible licensed professionals prepare technical materials as applicable. Determine the actual filing fees from current city schedules and staff, not the older packet's example amounts.

## 3. Submit application and pay filing fees

Westminster says to submit the application and specified materials to Planning Division and pay applicable fees at filing. Its older packet specifies printed plan sets and digital media; the city's newer site advertises **Westminster Build**. Confirm the current Planning Division filing channel, file formats, plan counts, and payment instructions directly.

## 4. Completeness review

City staff checks whether required information is present. An incomplete application cannot proceed. Record city requests and corrections, responsible parties, dates, and versions. The assistant should flag missing information and assemble revised drafts; a human approves all responses.

## 5. Entitlement / environmental / interagency review

Following completeness, the city evaluates applicable zoning and other requirements, CEQA, external agency reviews such as OCFA, and public notice or outreach if applicable. The decision maker may be the Community Development Director, Planning Commission, or City Council depending on entitlement. Confirm decision authority and whether public notice or a hearing is required.

## 6. Decision and conditions

Track the decision and any conditions of approval. Some decisions may be appealed. Approval of the planning entitlement does **not** itself authorize construction.

## 7. Building plan check and permits

After planning approval, prepare detailed construction plans and required engineering, energy and technical documents. Submit to Building Division for plan check, obtain required approvals and permits, then proceed with construction and inspections. Final occupancy follows successful inspections.

## Key unresolved questions

- Is Development Review the correct and only entitlement for the specific parcel and housing proposal?
- Which requirements are mandatory versus conditional for this project?
- What is the current Planning Division online versus in-person submission process?
- What are the current fees, plan-format requirements and agency review charges?
- Does the project qualify for density bonus, streamlining, or other affordable-housing provisions?

No signature, professional certification, agency determination, or submission should be generated or represented as completed by the AI.
